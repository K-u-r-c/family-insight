# The sample family

Want to look around before entering your own family? Family Insight comes with a made-up family
of **25 people across five generations**.

On the welcome screen, click **Try a sample family**:

<Screenshot name="welcome-annotated" alt="The welcome screen with five numbered buttons: 1 New family tree, 2 Open tree, 3 Import from CSV, 4 Import from GEDCOM, 5 Try a sample family" />

1. **New family tree** – start your own tree ([Your first family tree](./first-tree)).
2. **Open tree…** – open a `.finsight` file you saved before.
3. **Import from CSV…** – start from a spreadsheet ([Import from CSV](./csv-import)).
4. **Import from GEDCOM…** – bring a tree from another genealogy program ([GEDCOM](./gedcom)).
5. **Try a sample family** – open the sample family.

## What's in it

The sample is the (fictional) Kowalski family from Łódź, with their relatives the Wiśniewskis,
the Lis family and the Zając family. It shows what Family Insight can handle:

- **Five generations**, from Józef Kowalski (born about 1898) to his great-great-granddaughter
  Lena (born 2021).
- **A second marriage and half-siblings:** Piotr Kowalski married twice – Michał is his son from
  the first marriage, Jakub and Natalia from the second.
- **Birth surnames** (maiden names), for example Anna Kowalska née Wiśniewska.
- **Approximate and partial dates** such as `~1898` (about 1898) and `1935` (year only).
- **Places, notes and life status** – living and deceased people.
- **A root person** (🏠): Jan Kowalski. The [ancestor and descendant views](./view-modes) start
  from him.

Things to try:

- Click **Piotr Kowalski** and look at his family in the details panel.
- Switch to the **Ancestors** view, or search for **lodz** to find everyone born in Łódź.
- Open the **Statistics** (📊) to see the most common names and places.

## Is it saved?

The sample opens as a new, **unsaved** tree. Change anything you like – nothing is stored until you
save it with **File → Save as…**. If you close it after making changes, Family Insight asks whether to save them;
choose **Don't save** to throw them away.

# Relationship calculator

The relationship calculator tells you how two people in the tree are related – in words, such as
“first cousin once removed” or “great-aunt” – and shows the line of people that connects them.

## Opening the calculator

- Click the **Relationship calculator** button on the right of the toolbar, or choose **View →
  Relationship calculator…** in the menu bar. The selected person and the
  [root person](./view-modes#root-person) are filled in for you.
- Right-click a card and choose **Find relationship…** to start from that person.

## Choosing the people

The calculator reads like a sentence: the **Person** on the left is related to the person on the
right (**In relation to**). Click either box to choose someone from the list – type to search. The
⇄ button swaps the two.

The result appears straight away, in the language of the app – for example “Michał Kowalski is the
first cousin of Aleksandra Lis”. Below it is the **path**: everyone from the first person to the
second, through their closest common ancestor.

Some people are related in more than one way – double cousins, for example, whose fathers are
brothers and whose mothers are sisters. Then **All relationships** lists every one of them; click
one to see its path.

## Showing the path on the tree

Click **Show on tree**. The calculator closes, the tree zooms to the path, the people on it are
outlined and everyone else fades. If someone on the path is hidden in the current
[view mode](./view-modes) or in a [collapsed branch](./navigation#collapse), the tree shows the
whole family first. To hide the path, click **×** in the bar above the tree or press **Esc**.

## Relationship to the root person {#root-person}

When the tree has a [root person](./view-modes#root-person), the details of everyone else say how
they are related to them, for example “The granddaughter of Piotr Kowalski”. Click that line to
open the calculator for the two of them.

## How relationships are named

- **Blood relatives** are named after their closest common ancestors: parents, grandparents and
  great-great-grandparents, children and grandchildren, siblings and half-siblings, aunts and
  uncles, nephews and nieces, and cousins of any degree, removed any number of times.
- **Partners, in-laws and step relatives** – husband, father-in-law, sister-in-law, stepmother,
  stepbrother – are related through a marriage or another partnership.
- **Adoptive and foster** lines are named as such: adoptive mother, foster son.
- **Every language uses its own words.** In Polish, for example, a father’s brother is a _stryj_ and
  a mother’s brother a _wuj_, so switching the app [language](./settings) changes the answer, not
  just its translation.
- **Very distant relatives**, for whom no language has a word, are described by their common
  ancestors and the number of generations – for example “… are related through the common ancestors
  Jan Kowalski and Maria Nowak, 13 generations up and 11 down”.

If the calculator says that two people are not related, check that the parents linking them are in
the tree ([Relationships](./relationships)).
